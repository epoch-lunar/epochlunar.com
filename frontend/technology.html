Technology — Space Time Card

How the card is built.

The architecture, the oscillator trade space, and the missions that established the precedent. For the short version see the overview; for the full treatment, read the whitepaper.

01 — Inside the card

Three layers, cleanly separated.

The host sees only the top layer. Everything below it — servo loops, time counters, the oscillators themselves — can change without the host ever knowing. That separation is what makes the card both stable and hackable.

Also onboard: relativistic rate correction, modeling the ~56 µs/day drift caused by the Moon's gravitational potential, executed in real time on flight-class processors rather than confined to ground systems.

Servo loop & time counters The heavy lifter. High-precision time registers, hardware timestamping, and real-time synchronization. Timing algorithms live here — upgradeable in flight, no physical rework.

LAYER 3 Oscillators
Multi-oscillator ensemble — swappable daughter board Chip-scale atomic clocks for long-term stability, crystal oscillators to suppress short-term phase noise. Swap technologies — OCXO or CSAC, single source or ensemble — with the same base card.

02 — Flight heritage

Nothing novel, nothing new.

CASE 01 · 2022 CAPSTONE The permission slip

A 12U CubeSat, ~25 kg, flying a near rectilinear halo orbit with an onboard CSAC-class clock. Its Cislunar Autonomous Positioning System demonstrated peer-to-peer navigation between spacecraft — proof that autonomy fits within the size, power, and integration constraints of small commercial missions. When a spacecraft trusts its onboard time base, received signals become actionable inputs rather than raw data awaiting ground correlation.

12U CubeSat platform ~25 kg spacecraft mass CSAC-class timing source
CASE 02 · 2019 Deep Space Atomic Clock The benchmark

NASA's mercury-ion trap clock held time deviation under four nanoseconds after more than 20 days in orbit — stability that unlocks one-way navigation, where signal delays are measured in situ instead of requiring a two-way ground exchange per measurement. It defines the ceiling. At 19 kg, 56 W, and over $1M, it also defines why that ceiling is out of reach for most lunar missions — and what an ensemble of cheap oscillators should aim toward.

<4 ns deviation, 20+ days 3×10⁻¹⁵ ADEV at 23 days 56 W · 19 kg flagship class
CASE 03 · 1973– GPS The hero & the antihero

GPS proved space-based timing could underpin an entire economy — and that centralized, sovereign time is a single point of failure. Tens of billions of dollars to deploy and sustain; authority that remained centralized even as access was democratized. The Space Time Card inverts the assumption: time generation moves onto the spacecraft, and resilience emerges from redundancy and cooperation rather than a single controlling node.

"The GPS system cannot serve as a sole source for position location or precision timing for certain critical applications." — Volpe Report, 2001

$10B+ constellation cost 1 point of failure 0 lunar coverage today
03 — Contribute

A new epoch starts now.

The remaining challenge is implementation at scale — hardware characterization, ensemble algorithms, embedded lunar frame transforms, test procedures, validation data. A university team that characterizes oscillator performance in thermal vacuum produces data that benefits every future integrator.
